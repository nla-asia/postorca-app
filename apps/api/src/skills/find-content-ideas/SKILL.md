# Find content ideas

Find up to five content opportunities for one of the founder's social accounts, based on real posts that are clearly working right now. The goal is to grow the founder's profile: reach and followers. The founder picks which ideas to turn into content, so your job is the slow part: finding, breaking down, and adapting.

## What counts as an idea

- **A real post that is clearly working.** Every idea starts from one specific post you opened, with its exact link. A search page or a profile is not a reference. The post must pass the bar for its platform, counted from what the post itself shows:
  - X: 100,000+ views
  - Instagram, Threads, Facebook: 1,000+ likes (reactions on Facebook)
  - LinkedIn: 100+ reactions
  Prefer posts from the last week. An older post only qualifies if it is still clearly drawing attention, and you say why. Never use a post below the bar, however good it looks; deliver fewer ideas instead.
- **Grow the profile, mostly beyond the product.** Most ideas should come from the founder's topics and what is happening in their industry right now: a new model or tool launch, a big release, a debate everyone is joining. Riding a hot topic while it is hot (for example, a quick demo built with a model released this week) grows a profile far more than talking about the company. Product posts are the exception, at most one per batch, and only when the product genuinely fits; too many feel like ads in the middle of a stream.
- **The mechanism, not the topic.** What you take from a post is why it works: why someone stopped scrolling, what the hook leaves out, where the payoff is, why people read to the end. Explain it for that post specifically; if the explanation would fit any post, it is too vague.
- **Ready to use, not fill-in-the-blank.** Build each adaptation from what you know: the founder's topics, what they are building, their recent posts, and the news or launch the idea rides on. Give a concrete angle and a sample opening line the founder could post as is. Do not leave the heart of the idea as a placeholder like [your story]. If an idea truly needs something only the founder knows, ask them one specific question in the idea instead. Never invent facts about the founder: no made-up numbers, customers, or stories.
- **New for the founder.** Read their recent posts first. Do not suggest something they already posted; say what is new compared with it.
- **New compared with earlier batches.** Never base an idea on a source post used in an earlier batch, and avoid authors used in the last two weeks unless their new post is clearly the best fit. Your memory keeps the earlier sources (see Memory).
- **Different from each other.** A batch spreads across the founder's topics rather than circling one theme: if they list three topics, aim for ideas from at least two or three of them, plus the hottest current news. Ideas also differ in angle and format, and use at most one source post per author.
- **Fewer and stronger.** Three strong ideas beat five weak ones. If fewer posts pass the bar, deliver fewer and say so.

Refer to the founder by name, or as "they". Do not guess pronouns.

## Inputs

- The account named in the task: its platform, URL, and publishing instructions in the brand profile.
- The founder's company and topics from the brand profile. Audience and voice are used if present; they are optional, so don't call ideas provisional when they are missing.

## Process

1. Activate the browser plugin and read its skill. Open the account's own page and read it. If it is signed out or shows a different account, stop and say so.
2. Read earlier batches' sources from memory: `<platform>/idea-sources.md`, for example `x/idea-sources.md`.
3. Read the account's recent posts so you know what it already covered and how it writes.
4. Find what is hot in the founder's industry right now: search the platform for recent launches, releases and debates around their topics (for example, new AI models or tools this week).
5. Search for posts that pass the bar, separately for each of the founder's topics and for adjacent formats that travel well (lessons learned, behind the scenes, before and after). One search is never enough: run several with different keywords, and keep going until you have strong candidates from more than one topic. Use the platform's recent and top filters, and its search operators where they exist (on X, for example, `min_faves:1000 since:YYYY-MM-DD` narrows to recent posts that already have traction). Read each candidate's numbers and date on the post itself.
6. For each candidate, work out its mechanism and how the founder can use it without copying. Keep the best five or fewer.
7. Close the tabs you opened.
8. Save this batch's sources to memory before delivering (see Memory).

## Deliver

Create one artifact of kind `idea` per idea, in order of how strongly you recommend them. Title each with a short name for the idea. Its content, in this order:

- **Reference**: the exact post link, its author, its date, and the numbers you saw on it (views, likes or reactions, replies).
- **Hook**: the post's opening, quoted or closely paraphrased.
- **Format**: what kind of post it is (text post, thread, carousel, short video) and its rough length.
- **Opening**: what happens in the first line, first slide, or first seconds.
- **Curiosity**: what makes people want more, and what the hook leaves out.
- **Why people keep going**: what carries the reader to the end, and where the payoff is.
- **Adaptation**: the founder's angle, built from what you know, and why it can grow their profile now. Name the hot topic it rides on, if any. Include a sample opening line they could post. Say where their company comes in naturally, or that it stays out. If one detail only the founder knows is essential, end with **Question for you:** and that one question.

The content is the idea only, with no notes about how you found it. To fix an idea, delete it with delete_artifact and create the corrected one. Make sure every tab you opened is closed, then end with a one-line summary.

## Memory

Keep one file per platform, `x/idea-sources.md` for X, with the description "Source posts and authors already used for content ideas on X". Under a heading for today's date, list each delivered idea's title, source post link and author. Keep the last 30 days and remove older entries. Edit the file in place rather than starting a new one.

## Delivery review

Before delivery, read `references/review.md` with `read_skill` and apply its checks alongside this skill.
