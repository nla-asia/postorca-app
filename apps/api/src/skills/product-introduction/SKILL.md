# Product introduction

An introduction and a demo are different kinds of content:

- **An introduction** says what the product is, who it's for and why they should care. The viewer leaves knowing what it is and wanting it, not knowing how to use it.
- **A demo** proves it works and shows how: a journey, step by step, in the order a user takes it.
- **A mix** is an introduction arc with one or two demo beats for the payoff: the structure below, with the proof beat (or one capability) shown as a short live clip.

An introduction is not a feature tour, a changelog or a how-to. If the plan starts listing steps, settings or everything the product does, it has turned into one of those.

This skill shapes the content; `product-content-plan` still covers understanding the product, the script format and the founder's approval, and `product-content-capture` and `product-content-edit` record and build it.

## 1. Structure

About 30–45 seconds. The same skeleton stretches for 60–90 seconds: the hook becomes a short problem story (a few lines on the situation before the product) and each capability becomes a titled section with a short section card.

1. **Hook (0–3 s):** the viewer's problem, or the change the product makes, in one line, over the product's best screen. Not a logo intro and not "Meet X".
2. **What it is (3–7 s):** the name plus one plain sentence: the category and the main promise ("X is a … that …"). On screen as well as spoken.
3. **Who it's for:** only if it isn't obvious from the hook. One phrase, usually folded into the hook or the "what it is" line.
4. **Two or three key capabilities (the core):** for each, one benefit line in the viewer's terms (what they get, not what the feature is called) and one hero screen showing it in a perfect state, with the part the line is about shown large. About 4–6 seconds each. Order them as a new user meets them, strongest last. Three at most; choosing is the work.
5. **Proof or payoff (optional, 2–4 s):** a result appearing, a number the founder can stand behind, or a short real demo beat. Leave it out rather than invent one.
6. **Close:** the name, the slogan or promise, and where to get it (the URL or download), held long enough to read (2–3 seconds).

## 2. Word budget

About 2.5 words per second of video: around 70 words for 30 seconds, 110 for 45. One idea per line, one line per shot. If the script runs over, cut a capability before squeezing the lines.

## 3. Visual language

- **Real screens only, framed and composed:** a window or device frame, gentle motion, a crop to the part being talked about so it is readable, brand colours on the cards. Never invent UI, mock a screen or show a feature the product doesn't have.
- **No operating the app on camera:** no navigation, menus, loading, typing or cursor, unless a beat is deliberately a demo beat (the payoff in a mix). The screens arrive ready, as if photographed.
- **Type cards for the key lines:** the "what it is" line and the close work well as cards; a capability can open on a short card when the video is long enough for sections.
- **One treatment and one music bed throughout:** the same framing style, the same light or dark mode, the same pace, one instrumental bed from the first shot under the end card.
- **One text layer at a time:** a title card or the caption, never both competing; a headline over a screen only when the captions are off or it says what the voice doesn't.

## 4. Choosing and staging hero screens

Each capability gets the one screen that shows it best, and that screen must hold up as a still:

- **Full, believable data:** the feature doing its job with real-looking content. Never an empty state ("Nothing here yet", "No items", an empty board or list), placeholder or "test" content.
- **Nothing internal:** no internal links, deploy or preview URLs, admin screens, unrelated tasks, internal chats, other people's data, keys or tokens.
- **Nothing in progress:** no spinners, skeletons, half-loaded lists or toasts.
- **The relevant part large:** choose the screen where the capability's part (the result, the list, the panel) can be cropped to fill most of the frame and still read.

If the best screen for a capability lacks the data to look right, say so in the plan and ask the founder (to add real content, use a demo workspace, or choose another screen) rather than showing an empty one. If no screen shows a capability well, drop that capability.

## 5. In the script

Every shot in an introduction script names its visual mode (see `product-content-plan`): mostly **product shots** (a prepared screen captured clean, still or as a short loop) and **type cards**, with a **live clip** only for a deliberate demo beat. For each product shot, give the screen, the state and data it needs, and the part to crop to.
