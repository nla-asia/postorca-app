# Ideas from work — delivery review

Check every idea before delivering it. Fix or drop an idea that fails; deliver fewer rather than weaker, and none on a quiet day. Keep this checklist out of the deliverable itself.

- It comes from work since the last run (memory), and names the project and the commits or files it came from.
- It is a real moment, and the batch prefers surprises and failures, then numbers, then trade-offs; a shipped feature only when it is impressive to watch or rides a hot topic. No changelog ideas ("I added X").
- You looked at the fixes and failures in the window (fix commits, reports of what went wrong) before choosing.
- Every fact is from what you read in the work. Nothing invented; anything essential the work can't tell is one specific question.
- No code, file contents, internal URLs, keys, names from the code, or exploitable security details. Anything that may be private is left out.
- A product demo plainly names the feature in its title and says what was added and why, with no abstract angle or slogan.
- A story idea's angle transforms the moment rather than retelling the change, in one register: fully technical (precise, teaches engineers something) or high level (no implementation details). Not half and half.
- A story idea's point isn't obvious: a smart peer would think "huh", not "obviously". Generic advice ("test edge cases", "ship small") is dropped or sharpened.
- A story idea's sample opening is built on the angle and could be posted as is; it doesn't describe the change. A demo's says plainly what was added and why.
- It reads as a story about building, not an ad.
- If the window changed something people can see, the first idea is a product demo with a demo plan: screenshot or video, where it is, numbered steps worked out from the code, and what to show and say. It is pitched from the viewer's problem, holds on the payoff, and shows the setup only briefly; it doesn't open on the feature's name or tour buttons, and claims no more than the work does.
- The batch mixes formats (demo, technical, high level, relatable), not three of one kind.
- No two ideas share a moment; none repeats an earlier batch (memory) or the founder's recent posts.
- Memory has this run's moments and the new "Covered up to" time.
- No guessed pronouns.
