---
name: video-product-demo
description: "Demonstrate the product through real screens and steps, with a result-first hook."
---

Read video-create. A demo shows how the product is used, in the order a user meets its steps. For a thin brief, choose the platform's usual duration, first-person narration and captions; explain these defaults. Prefer 15–25 seconds for Reels/TikTok, up to 35 for LinkedIn, around 8–10 shots for 20 seconds.

Open on the supported payoff or pain removed, spoken and on screen. Put when/where it is used next. Give every supplied step a beat of 2–3 seconds, describing what someone does or gets; use short 2–5 word step labels. Then show the outcome and close on the exact URL or name. Say and show the product name at least once; never speak its URL. Avoid inventing personal usage beyond what the founder supplies.

Use real screen recordings whenever possible, then real screenshots. With no screenshots or recordings yet, read the product-demo-capture skill first: it records what it can and guides the founder for the rest.

Build the demo from the recorded clips, one or more scenes per clip. Before planning, preview a few frames across each clip (a scene with only that clip, at its start, middle and end) and note when each action starts, when the result appears and where the useful regions are. Cut each scene to its action: start just before the click or typing and end when the result is on screen; drop setup, cursor wandering and dead air. Speed up waits (loading, generation, long typing) 2–4× instead of cutting them out when the wait itself shows the work; keep clicks and the payoff at 1×. Hold the payoff 2–3 seconds with hold_seconds so it stays readable. Use focus to crop away browser tabs, address bar and debugging banners and to zoom on the control in use; a consecutive scene that picks up the same clip where the previous one stopped continues its shot and can move the focus. Screenshots fill gaps a clip doesn't cover. Read all supplied screenshots once and note their refs, screen type, existing frames, visible features and useful region boxes as fractions x/y/width/height. Visible controls are evidence; unsupported results, prices or feelings are not. Skip empty/setup screens unless they support a beat. Obtain available screenshots with existing browser tools or read_file; if access is missing, explain what the founder must supply rather than pretending capture happened.

Choose 9:16 for a named Reel/TikTok/Short or phone app. For desktop screens, prefer 16:9 for X, YouTube and LinkedIn or square/4:5 for feeds unless the brief specifies otherwise. When the founder posts to both X and Instagram, export a 16:9 and a 9:16 version from the same scenes and voiceover: change only format, then preview both, since a focus box that fits landscape may need tightening for portrait. Deliver each export separately. Show a whole desktop screen once, then focus or highlight each relevant control in portrait videos. fit contain preserves a whole screenshot. Device browser/laptop/phone frames are for bare screen content only: never double a screenshot's own window frame. Focus boxes should contain complete elements and labels; highlights retain location context while dimming the rest. Consecutive scenes may walk through regions on one screenshot. Limit stickers to 2–4 meaningful beats and keep them clear of the interface.

When real screens are unavailable, generated scenes may show hands, a soft screen, the setting or use around the product. Never fabricate its UI. Vary framing; disclose stand-ins and recommend real screens. Check every step's timing and region in previews, keep average shots below 3 seconds (at most 3.5 except the closing beat and a held payoff), and verify the final name/URL exactly. Avoid product-name openers, long feature slides, stickers over controls and narration of irrelevant example data.
