---
name: product-content-edit
description: "Produce the approved product video from its script and visual plan: recordings, product shots, type, illustrations, footage or animation, with captions, voiceover and music."
---

Read video-create. A demo shows how the product is used, in the order a user meets its steps; an introduction says what the product is and why it matters, using the approved visual treatment: type, illustrations, footage, animation and optional product shots. Planning guidance lives in `product-content-plan`, file `references/product-introduction.md`, readable with `read_skill`. For a thin brief, choose the platform's usual duration, first-person narration and captions; explain these defaults. Prefer 15–25 seconds for Reels/TikTok, up to 35 for LinkedIn, around 8–10 shots for 20 seconds (4–6 with screen recordings).

With an approved script, follow it shot by shot: each shot becomes one or more scenes, in its order, with its on-screen text as the scene's `text` and its voiceover as `says`; don't drop or reword either. Without one, open on the supported payoff or pain removed, spoken and on screen. Put when/where it is used next. Give every supplied step a beat of 2–3 seconds, describing what someone does or gets; use short 2–5 word step labels. Then show the outcome and close on an end card (a scene with text and no media): the product's name, a subtitle saying what it does, and the URL as its label when known, held 2–3 seconds. With two or more flows, introduce each with a short section card (its label the step, its title what happens) so the viewer knows what they are about to see. Say and show the product name at least once; never speak its URL. Avoid inventing personal usage beyond what the founder supplies. A launch or demo video carries a quiet instrumental music bed under the voice (read video-music; upbeat for a launch, calm for a walkthrough), so the cut never drops to silence between lines or under the end card.

**An introduction** follows its approved visual modes. Product screens are optional: use supplied or approved generated assets for illustration and footage, and compose type and animation with the available video tools. Do not replace these choices with a screen tour. When the plan includes product shots, type cards or a live demo beat:

- **Product shots:** a still is an `image_file` scene with `duration_seconds` (about 4–6 s per capability); a short loop is a `video_file` scene. Crop to the part the line is about with `focus` (fractions of the image), shaped close to the format (about 16:9 for a landscape video) so the screen fills most of the frame and its text reads; never show a whole desktop window small. Use `device` only for bare screen content, and `fit: "contain"` when a screen must show whole (it sits over a blurred copy of itself). Image scenes get a gentle drift from the renderer; for a pan, give consecutive scenes the same image with different `focus` boxes and the camera moves between them. No `highlight` or stickers over the screen unless one detail must stand out.
- **Type cards:** a scene with `text` and no media, on the brand background, with `subtitle` for the plain sentence of what it is and `label` for a small line such as the URL on the end card. Use them for the "what it is" line, section titles in a longer video, and the close.
- **One text layer at a time:** captions show the voiceover, so a card's title says the same as the line spoken over it (or the voice pauses), and product shots carry no `text` sticker that competes with the caption.
- **Cuts and fades:** scenes cut straight to each other (the tool has no crossfades between scenes; cards and focus moves animate in on their own), so keep one visual treatment and let the music carry continuity: one bed from the first shot under the end card, with `fade_in_seconds` and `fade_out_seconds` on it.
- **No dead frames:** the end card follows the last shot directly; no empty, black or held frame between them, and no hold or trailing seconds on the last shot. If the result reports a silent tail beyond the end card's 2–3 seconds, trim or move lines later until it goes.

For shots that show product behavior, use real screen recordings; for prepared product shots, use real screenshots or loops. If those screen assets are missing, read product-content-capture: it captures what it can and guides the founder for the rest. Type, illustration, footage and animation need no product screen capture.

Build the demo from the recorded clips, one or more scenes per clip. Before planning, read each recording with video_clip_text: it says what is on screen at the start and when each piece of text appears or goes, which is when each action lands (a total updates, a status appears, a button gives way to its result). Preview a few frames to see how it looks. Plan each scene from that timeline: start it just before what its line is about appears, and zoom in with focus_on, naming the one or two pieces of text the line is about as they read on screen (the button being clicked, the total, the badge that appears) at that moment, rather than estimating a box by eye. A close-up keeps enough of the app around what it shows that the viewer knows where it is; go tight only when the detail is the point on its own (a total, a status), and check each shot's zoom in the preview. Cut each scene to its action: start just before the click or typing and end when the result is on screen; drop setup, cursor wandering and dead air, and any mistake in the take (a wrong page opened and backed out of, a typo corrected, a hesitation): each scene covers only the right action. Speed up waits (loading, generation, long typing) 2–4× instead of cutting them out when the wait itself shows the work; keep clicks and the payoff at 1×. Hold the payoff 2–3 seconds with hold_seconds so it stays readable. A whole desktop recording is unreadable on a phone and small even in landscape: show it whole at most once, and give every other clip scene a focus box on the part in action (the field being typed in, the button, the result appearing), large enough to hold complete elements. Focus also crops away browser tabs, the address bar and debugging banners. Previews report text_height_px, how tall the app's text shows on a phone: under about 26 px it can't be read, so focus on fewer and shorter lines, or have the shot recorded again with the app zoomed in. A scene's captions go to the top when the lower part of the frame holds what its line is about, and off when the scene already shows the same words, such as a clip of a captioned video. A consecutive scene that picks up the same clip where the previous one stopped continues its shot and can move the focus. Screenshots fill gaps a clip doesn't cover. Read all supplied screenshots once and note their refs, screen type, existing frames, visible features and useful region boxes as fractions x/y/width/height. Visible controls are evidence; unsupported results, prices or feelings are not. Skip empty/setup screens unless they support a beat. Obtain available screenshots with existing browser tools or read_file; if access is missing, explain what the founder must supply rather than pretending capture happened.

Say plainly what was added and why, the way a product page does; no slogans or abstract concepts. For a demo without an approved opening treatment, the first scene carries the hook as its `text`, on screen as well as spoken; if it would cover something, place it elsewhere or use a focus box. Take the viewer's problem from the brief, commits or notes; if none states one, open on the payoff. Pass the script as `voiceover_script` so captions use its exact words.

Keep the picture and the words together: give each clip scene `says`, the first words of the voiceover it goes with, and the tool starts the scene when they are spoken. If a tool note says the last scene was stretched well past the payoff hold, or reports slow pacing, the script is too long for the clips.

The approved plan names the platform and shape; build for it:

- **16:9 (X, LinkedIn, YouTube):** real screens recorded in use. Show the whole app once to set the scene, then focus on the action.
- **9:16 (Reels, TikTok, Shorts):** a phone app fills the frame as it is. A desktop or web app is shown through its pieces: each scene focuses on one complete element (a card, a message, a list, a dialog, a result) with fit contain, so it fills the width and still reads as the app. Never a box that cuts through text or the element, and never the whole window. Keep its text at 32 px or more by text_height_px. The hook is on screen in the first second, and a short line of on-screen text per scene carries the story, kept clear of the element (an exception to the sticker limit below); captions go to the top or off where they would cover it.
- **4:5 and 1:1 (feeds):** a desktop screen fits with room for a line of text, but its text stays small, so focus on the part in action.

When the founder posts to more than one platform, build each shape from its own scenes and preview each before exporting: a portrait version of a desktop video is made from its pieces, not the landscape cut with tighter boxes. Deliver each export separately. fit contain preserves a whole screenshot. Device browser/laptop/phone frames are for bare screen content only: never double a screenshot's own window frame. Focus boxes should contain complete elements and labels; highlights retain location context while dimming the rest. Consecutive scenes may walk through regions on one screenshot. Limit stickers to 2–4 meaningful beats and keep them clear of the interface.

When real screens are unavailable, generated scenes may show hands, a soft screen, the setting or use around the product. Never fabricate its UI. Vary framing; disclose stand-ins and recommend real screens. Check every step's timing and region in previews, keep average shots below 3 seconds (at most 3.5 except the closing beat and a held payoff), or at most 5 seconds for real screen recordings, which take longer to read (an introduction's product shots run 4–6 seconds each), and verify the final name/URL exactly. Avoid long feature slides, stickers over controls and narration of irrelevant example data.
